I have studied religion and demography worldwide, inequality in how families form, children’s health and education in Texas, voter access, and most recently eviction and debt collection in the civil courts. That work has drawn on national censuses, Houston parcel maps, and county court dockets.

When Real Debt Collection Lawsuits Look Like Scams: The Problem with Pocket Filing in Minnesota

Minnesota lets a debt collector serve a lawsuit up to a year before filing it with the court, so defendants cannot confirm the case is real. A review of 1,000 cases found most plaintiffs file within two months anyway.

43 states have online voter registration. Why not Texas? with Shannon Carter

Florida registration data from 2017 to 2024 shows no independent partisan effect from online registration. County population size drove the trends instead.

Most people sued for debt never get their day in court. We can fix that.

People sued for debt are 2.1 to 3.7 times more likely to take part in their case where courts require no formal written answer. Dropping California's answer requirement could bring 5,000 to 12,000 more defendants into the process each year.

Consumer Debt Collection Lawsuits in Wisconsin, 2018-2024 with Divia Kallattil

Wisconsin courts saw nearly 100,000 debt collection filings in 2024, most brought by national debt buyers and banks. Black residents were sued at 1.86 times the rate of white residents.

January AdvisorsDecember 2025

Could stronger wage garnishment protections mean fewer debt collection lawsuits?

States with stronger wage garnishment protections see fewer debt collection filings per capita. Raising the nine states graded F to a C could mean roughly 128,000 fewer cases a year.

Consumer Debt Cases Are Surging Again—and One Company Is Leading the Charge with Divia Kallattil

Debt collection filings matched or passed pre-pandemic levels in 2024 and 2025. LVNV Funding's volume rose 350% since 2019 and now makes up 18% of all filings in the sample.

January AdvisorsMarch 2025
How campaign donations affect indigent defense in Harris County with Brian Barr, Jeff Reichman

Harris County judges assigned about twice as many indigent defense cases to attorneys who donated to their campaigns. Donor attorneys got no better outcomes than non-donors, and public defenders outperformed appointed counsel.

January AdvisorsAugust 2024

Measuring racial disparities in consumer debt lawsuits

In Michigan, Minnesota, and Oregon, Black and Latino residents faced debt collection lawsuits at two to three times the rate of non-Hispanic white residents. The gap held in higher-income neighborhoods.

Third-party debt collectors and debt buyers dominate Oregon's civil courts, few Oregonians participate in debt cases against them with Divia Kallattil

Debt collection cases made up 52% of Oregon's circuit court civil docket and reached more than 50,000 residents a year. Fewer than 4% of defendants responded and fewer than 1% had a lawyer.

Evictions in Houston After Hurricane Harvey

Eviction filings dipped right after Hurricane Harvey and then spiked. Flooded garden-style apartment complexes saw a 29% increase in evictions between 2016 and 2019.

A scary new era for evictions in Harris County

Harris County recorded close to 80,000 eviction filings in 2022, well above the pre-pandemic norm of 55,000 to 60,000. That ranked third nationally against historical averages.

Essay: 5 minutes per eviction hearing? Why Harris County should redraw court maps.

Harris County justice of the peace precincts have not been redrawn since 1973. Some courts have about a minute per eviction case while others have fifteen.

Precinct 5 carries close to ten times the population and caseload of Precinct 6. A simulation of 1,000 alternative precinct maps produced more balanced caseloads than the map in use.

Galveston has a housing crisis on multiple fronts

Median home prices in Galveston rose 80% over a decade and short-term rentals more than doubled after 2019. About 800 fewer families with children now live on the island.

Houston-based care coordination pilot program improves health outcomes

Houston's C3 pilot completed 69% of its care pathways using dedicated coordinators. Scaling to 1,000 clients could save $236,000 to $600,000 in avoided emergency room visits.

First Results of the CDC Eviction Moratorium in Tarrant County

The CDC moratorium cut Tarrant County eviction filings to 57% of the historical average. Only 27% of eligible tenants filed a declaration, and landlords still won 35% of the cases where one was filed.

American Community Survey data identified the Houston neighborhoods with the most historically undercounted residents. Density rather than percentage share determined the best mural sites, which put Gulfton ahead of Second Ward.

Top 5 Takeaways About the Vote Your Way Campaign

About half of Harris County voters used a polling place outside their assigned precinct under countywide voting. Among voters ages 18 to 29 the share was 62%.

January AdvisorsDecember 2019

Serving Veterans After Hurricane Harvey: Five Key Findings

An estimated 100,000 Gulf Coast veterans applied for FEMA aid after Harvey and 60% reported home damage. Two years later about two-thirds still had no flood insurance.

Children ages 0 to 4 face the highest census undercount risk. A 1% Texas undercount would cost more than $3 billion in Medicaid and CHIP funding over a decade.

Mapping suspected illegal massage businesses against Texas schools found more than 35,000 students attending class within 1,000 feet of one. Online ad data showed trafficking activity rising after Hurricane Harvey.

Making Texas Hard to Count: Citizenship & Census 2020

About 22% of Texans live with a non-citizen. If every such household skipped the census over the citizenship question, Texas would lose more than $100 billion in federal funding over ten years.

Why Houston Will Be Hard to Count in Census 2020

Harris County's low response score of 24% runs five points above the national average, driven by its high renter share, diversity, and young population. A 1% undercount would cost roughly $54 million a year.

January AdvisorsNovember 2018

Did Late Openings at Nine Polling Locations in Harris County Affect Voter Turnout?

Harris County turnout rose from 34% in 2014 to 52% in 2018. Precincts tied to the nine late-opening polling places gained turnout faster than the county overall, up 93% against 56%.

Without CHIP reauthorization, uninsured Texas children would climb from about 650,000 to more than one million by the end of March 2018. Over half of Texas CHIP enrollees live in six counties.

School districts with the top food programs for low-income students in Texas with Jenny Eyer

Rio Grande Valley and El Paso districts led the 2016-17 school meal rankings, with 80% to 90% of low-income students eating school breakfast and lunch. San Antonio ISD and Dallas ISD led among districts over 50,000 students.

Districts in the 58-county Harvey disaster area are represented by 44% of Texas House members and 58% of state senators. Nearly 3 million affected children fall under lawmakers from both parties.

Harvey delayed the school year for about 1.4 million students across 117 districts and nearly 1,800 campuses. Close to 60% of those students were economically disadvantaged.

Academic performance across Texas school districts

Only 14% of students in the average Texas district reached the Masters grade level standard on STAAR in 2015-16. Variation within large districts was wide, with Houston ISD campuses ranging from under 1% to above 80%.

Sub-Saharan Africa will be home to growing shares of the world's Christians and Muslims

Sub-Saharan Africa's share of the world's Christians is projected to rise from 26% in 2015 to more than 40% by 2060. Europe's share falls from nearly a quarter to 14%.

Pew Research CenterApril 2017
Why people with no religion are projected to decline as a share of the world's population with Michael Lipka

The religiously unaffiliated population will grow to 1.2 billion by 2060 but shrink from 16% to 13% of the world's people. The unaffiliated are older and have fewer children than religiously affiliated people.

Pew Research CenterApril 2017
The Changing Global Religious Landscape with Conrad Hackett, Marcin Stonawski, and others

Births to Muslim mothers are projected to outnumber births to Christian mothers by 2035. Only 10% of the world's newborns between 2010 and 2015 were born to religiously unaffiliated mothers.

Pew Research CenterApril 2017
Christians remain world's largest religious group, but they are declining in Europe with Conrad Hackett

Christians are 31% of the world's population and still the largest religious group. In Europe, Christian deaths outnumbered Christian births by nearly 6 million between 2010 and 2015.

Pew Research CenterDecember 2016

Muslims in sub-Saharan Africa are twice as likely as Christians to have no formal education

In sub-Saharan Africa, 65% of Muslim adults have no formal schooling. The figure for Christian adults is 30%.

Pew Research CenterDecember 2016
Religion and Education Around the World with Conrad Hackett, Michaela Potančoková, Marcin Stonawski

Jews have the most years of formal schooling of any major religious group, and Muslims and Hindus the fewest. The youngest religiously unaffiliated adults have now passed Christians in average years of schooling.

Pew Research CenterNovember 2016
Child marriage is rare in the U.S., though this varies by state with Aleksandra Sandstrom

About 57,800 Americans ages 15 to 17 were married in 2014, or five of every 1,000 in that age group. West Virginia and Texas had the highest rates.

Pew Research CenterMay 2016

Gender gap in religious service attendance has narrowed in U.S.

The gap between women's and men's weekly religious service attendance narrowed from 10 percentage points in the early 1970s to 6 points in the early 2010s. Women's attendance fell faster than men's.

The Gender Gap in Religion Around the World with Conrad Hackett, Caryle Murphy, Anne Fengyan Shi

Across 192 countries women are generally more religious than men. The pattern is strongest among Christians and largely absent among Muslims, except in mosque attendance, where men attend more.

Interfaith Marriage and Romantic Unions in the United States

The share of U.S. marriages joining partners from different religions roughly doubled from the 1960s to the 2000s. A growing share of those couples keep separate religious identities rather than one partner converting.
